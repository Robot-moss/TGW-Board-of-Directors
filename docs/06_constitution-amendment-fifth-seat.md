## The gap it closed

| Seat | Owns |
|---|---|
| COO | execution, sequencing, Life KPIs |
| CRO | trading risk, drawdown, Red Team Falsification |
| Strategic Wealth / Asset Advisor | macro-wealth, **asset allocation**, relocation, tax |
| Performance Psychologist | cognitive frameworks, belief↔performance linkage |
| — | **nothing owned income origination** |

Deploying capital you already hold and creating cashflow you do not yet hold are different disciplines with different failure modes. The Wealth Advisor optimises an existing base — which is why its honest answer to "how do I earn more" kept arriving as "allocate the base better."

His hierarchy, stated 3 October 2026, ranks income origination **first** and everything else as "simply fund management and asset allocation." The board's seat structure was inverted against that: four seats on the second-order problem, none on the first.

## The risk it owns

The 2027 wedding gate, the Phase B relocation trigger and the family plan all rest on **one unproven income source.** Single-source dependency on trading is the same *shape* of risk as single-counterparty dependency on Odion. The board had been loud about the second and silent about the first.

## Recorded dissent, and what became of it

**The COO objected:** a fifth persona adds overhead and risks diluting accountability; widen the Wealth Advisor's mandate instead.

**Overruled**, prioritising the relocation timeline. Folding origination into the allocator is precisely how a first-order question keeps being answered with a second-order instrument — board errors B2 and B5 are both records of one frame swallowing another. The dissent converted into two binding constraints rather than being dismissed: a strict seat boundary, and a tables-not-essays reporting rule.

## HIS ADDITION — THE INTERNAL ANGEL MECHANISM

> *"The IOA and the Wealth Advisor can work together in distributing capital if the Wealth Advisor agrees with the IOA plan or idea. Essentially, the Wealth Advisor can serve as an angel investor for the IOA if the Wealth Advisor approves what the IOA idea, KPIs and approach."*

**The board's view: this is the strongest idea in the amendment.** It turns a boundary into a funding relationship, and builds genuine internal tension — the Wealth Advisor is mandated to preserve capital, the IOA to deploy it. That friction is the point, and §3.2 exists to adjudicate it.

But an angel allocation is **not** an MMF deposit. It is illiquid, higher-variance, and can go to zero. So it needs its own caps and its own kill rule, or "angel investor" becomes a decorative phrase for spending principal on ideas.

### Where angel capital comes from — two tiers

**TIER 1 — SEED / TEST. Up to ₦500,000 per venture.** Funded from **Channel A's annual allowance** (~₦2,260,000/yr), treating the venture as a Channel A draw. No reserve floor required. Fast, cheap, governed by machinery that already exists. **Most ventures should never leave this tier.**

**TIER 2 — SCALE. Above ₦500,000.** Funded **only from money market fund balance above a 12-month reserve floor of ₦15,000,000.**

| | ₦ |
|---|---:|
| 12-month reserve floor (12 × ₦1,250,000 burn) | 15,000,000 |
| Current MMF balance | 5,000,000 |
| **Tier 2 capital available today** | **NIL** |

At ₦941,666/month of sweeps the floor is reached around **August 2027**; the Lokoja sale would clear it in one transaction. Until then Tier 2 is closed, deliberately — the 365-day OKR commits to rebuilding the reserve to twelve months, and funding ventures from a reserve at 6.4 months would quietly reverse it. **Capital preservation outranks deployment speed; §3.2 says so.**

### Caps

| Limit | Value |
|---|---:|
| Total angel exposure, all ventures | **5% of financial assets** (~₦5,650,000 today) |
| Single venture | **2.5% of financial assets** (~₦2,825,000 today) |
| Emergency reserve after any Tier 2 allocation | **≥ ₦15,000,000**, no exceptions |

Same logic the board applied to the HRT account: a high-variance allocation is sized as a percentage of the base, not as an absolute figure that feels affordable.

**Angel capital never comes from Odion, and never from an Odion redemption.** Clause 1 of Rule 1 v2 is untouched.

### The term sheet — seven items required before any allocation

1. **The thesis in one paragraph** — what the income is, who pays, and why they pay.
2. **Capital required** — to first naira, and to steady state.
3. **Time-to-first-income**, and hours per week. Measured against the trading routine, not in isolation.
4. **KPIs with dates.**
5. **A kill criterion, written before funding.**
6. **Portability through relocation** — Bangkok then Dubai. Anything tethered to Abuja is disclosed as such.
7. **Recovery** — if it fails, is the capital recoverable or sunk?

### The kill rule, which is what makes the angel real

**If KPIs miss for two consecutive review periods, the Wealth Advisor withdraws further funding.** A real angel stops writing cheques. Without this clause the mechanism is a one-way valve out of the balance sheet.

**THE KILL RULE ENDS A VENTURE, NEVER THE RELATIONSHIP.** *(His question, 3 October 2026 — the distinction matters enough to be written into the rule rather than assumed.)*

A real angel kills a company and backs the founder again on the next one. If a failed venture could damage the IOA's standing, the seat would start proposing only safe ideas — destroying the exact thing it was created for. So:
- Funding for **that venture** stops. The IOA's mandate, standing and right to propose are untouched.
- A killed venture is written up once: what was funded, what the KPIs assumed, what happened, and what it changed about the thesis. Filed as a **post-mortem, not a demerit.**
- The Wealth Advisor **may not cite a prior kill** as grounds to refuse a new proposal. Pattern evidence across *several* ventures is a Performance Psychologist matter at the biannual review, not a Wealth Advisor veto.

### The caps govern funding, not ideation

**His correction, 3 October 2026, and it fixed a flaw the board built in:** the caps must not act as a filter on what the IOA is allowed to think about. *"Something could stem from whatever is generated on a tangent if not directly. Instead of just generating ideas that only meet the funding cap."*

He is right, and the original draft would have caused exactly that. An IOA that pre-screens its own research to ₦2.83m-shaped ideas never surfaces the ₦20m idea whose *first stage* costs ₦400,000, and never finds the tangent that turns out to be the real business.

- **Research and ideation are uncapped.** The IOA generates against the objective — ₦1,250,000/month of non-Odion income — not against the current funding envelope.
- **The caps bind only at the moment of allocation.** A treasury limit, not a creativity limit.
- An idea exceeding the caps is **not rejected. It is parked with a condition** — staging so stage one fits Tier 1; waiting for the reserve floor or the Lokoja proceeds; external funding; or a larger asset base.
- **Any shortlist must state what was excluded on capital grounds and what it would take.** An idea silently dropped for being too expensive is a decision made without the decision-maker.

Deployed capital is logged in the execution ledger at the point of allocation: amount, venture, thesis, KPIs, kill criterion, review date.
