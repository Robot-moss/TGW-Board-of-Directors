### A3. The liquidity objection — partly unfounded

You are holding back ₦3m of the ₦8m for "subsequent cash liquidity for any unforeseen circumstances." That instinct is sound, but the premise behind it is not:

**A money market fund redeems in 24–48 hours with no penalty.** It is not a locked product. Piggyvest is not meaningfully more liquid — it is simply more *familiar*. The genuine difference between them is about one business day.

So the real question is not "how much can I afford to lock up" — nothing is being locked. It is: **how much cash do I need available same-day, today, before the bank opens tomorrow?** For most emergencies in Nigeria — medical, travel, family — 48 hours is fine. For a small class of them it is not.

**The board accepts ₦5m as your decision and does not contest it.** But the ₦3m retained should be understood as a *convenience buffer*, not a liquidity necessity, and it should shrink as your comfort with the instrument grows. Recommended target: ₦1.5m retained in Piggyvest by December, the rest deployed.

### C4. The honest counter-arguments

The board will not pretend this is costless.

**It was your father's.** That is a real consideration and the board does not treat it as irrational. But note what is actually being honoured: an asset in a city you do not live in, which you will not live in, producing ₦8,333 a month. If the intent of the inheritance was to provide for you, an asset earning 0.21% is not doing that. Capital honours its source by working, not by sitting still.

**Lokoja market liquidity is thin.** Kogi State has a far shallower secondary market than Abuja. A ₦47.5m asset may take 9–18 months to sell, and the achieved price may sit at the bottom of the range. **This is the strongest argument for starting the title work now rather than later** — the sale process cannot even begin until the title chain is complete, and both processes are slow. Run them in sequence and you are looking at two years.

**Appreciation foregone.** Lokoja property may appreciate. But at 20.54% compounding in an MMF, the property must appreciate more than 20% a year just to match — in a market with weak fundamentals. That is not a close call.

**The alternative — re-let at market.** Your Abuja flat was under-rented by 4.5x for a decade, so the Lokoja rent is probably also far below market. Even so: a realistic Lokoja 2-bed market rent of ₦600,000–800,000/yr on a ₦47.5m asset is a **1.3–1.7% yield.** Better than 0.21%, still not an investment. Re-letting is worth doing *while the property is being prepared for sale*, not instead of selling.

### C5. Board recommendation

**Sell the Lokoja flat. Begin title perfection immediately, because it gates everything.**

Dissent recorded: the COO notes that sale proceeds landing in 2027–28 may collide with short-let capex and relocation timing, and that a forced sale into a thin market destroys value. ## PART E — TITLE PERFECTION: THE GATING ITEM

You hold **Letters of Administration** in your name plus two siblings and an uncle. That is the hard part, and it is done. What remains is the transfer chain.

### E1. The chain

| Step | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Deed of Family Partition / Assignment | The instrument that vests each specific unit in each specific sibling, executed by the administrators | Your existing sibling agreement is evidence of intent, not a transfer |
| 2. Stamping | 1.5% stamp duty to FIRS | |
| 3. Minister's Consent (FCT) / Governor's Consent (Kogi) | Land Use Act requirement | Abuja goes through AGIS |
| 4. Registration | Recording at the land registry | This is what makes the interest enforceable against third parties |

**Timeline: 3–12 months. Cost: commonly 10–20% of value**, though family partitions of an administered estate are generally lighter than arm's-length transfers. Get a fixed-fee quote rather than accepting a percentage.

### E2. Instruction brief for the lawyer

Take this to counsel — ideally not the same lawyer holding the C of O, for reasons in E3:

> Estate of [father], died intestate January 2021. Letters of Administration granted to [three named siblings] and [uncle] as administrators. Estate includes a block of four 2-bedroom flats at Apo Resettlement, Abuja, and a 2-bedroom flat at Lokoja, Kogi State. C of O for the Abuja property is in the deceased's name.
>
> **Instructions:**
> 1. Conduct title searches on both properties and report any encumbrances.
> 2. Prepare a Deed of Family Partition vesting one specified Abuja unit in each of the four beneficiaries, and the Lokoja property in [David].
> 3. Attend to stamping, Minister's Consent (FCT) and Governor's Consent (Kogi).
> 4. Register both transfers and obtain registered title in each beneficiary's name.
> 5. Advise specifically on whether the intended commercial short-let use of the Abuja block requires any change of use consent, and on the FCTA's stated power to revoke land titles for unapproved commercial use of residential property.
>
> **Deliverables required:** fixed-fee quotation, itemised disbursements, and a written timeline with milestones.

### E3. On the C of O and the lawyer

Your explanation is accepted and the board's earlier framing was too aggressive. Leaving the C of O with your father's lawyer was a deliberate security decision taken when the circumstances of your father's death were unresolved and you had reason to fear for your own safety. That is not negligence; it is discretion under threat, and it was a joint family decision. Recovery is on request.

**One residual point, stated once and then dropped.** The same individual holds ₦80m of your capital, pays 100% of your income, and holds the family title deed. Each of those is defensible on its own. The board's concern is only the correlation: a single adverse event touching that individual touches all three at once. Retrieving the C of O costs nothing, is available on request, and removes one of the three legs. **Do it during the title work — counsel will need the original anyway.**
